# Sereinfolio

Sereinfolio is a non-divinatory reflection and storytelling activity. Players
use the names and visible details of physical tarot cards as starting points
for observation and imagination. The cards do not predict events, diagnose a
person, reveal hidden truth, or prescribe a decision.

Passing is always a complete turn. Nobody has to explain a pass or disclose a
personal experience.

## What you need

- One to eight players and a facilitator. A solo player can facilitate their
  own session.
- One complete 78-card tarot deck.
- A table where each drawn card can remain visible for its round.

Ludocairn supplies canonical card names only. Use the physical deck's imagery
for visual observation, and leave its guidebook closed during this activity.

## Set up the folio

1. Shuffle all 78 cards without separating major and minor arcana.
2. Agree on three rounds for a short session or five rounds for a longer one.
   Record the chosen length in the session notes.
3. Add every player to the tracker. Leave Reflection and Prompt notes empty,
   set Tone to `quiet`, and begin at round one.
4. Read the pass rule aloud: a player may pass at any point, with no follow-up
   question and no change to how the group treats them.
5. Decide whether cards drawn upside down will be turned upright. Orientation
   has no special meaning in Sereinfolio, so use one approach consistently.

## A round

Work clockwise from a new starting player each round. Complete all six parts
for one player before the next player draws.

### 1. Draw

The player draws the top card and places it face up. The facilitator says only
its canonical name, such as `The Star`, `Ten of Cups`, or `Knight of Swords`.
Do not read or summarize a deck guidebook meaning.

### 2. Observe

Allow about twenty silent seconds. The player may name one directly visible
detail: a color, count, posture, direction, repeated shape, distance, or
relationship between figures. An abstract or minimally illustrated deck is
also suitable; the card name alone can be the observed detail.

### 3. Choose a tone

The player chooses a tone and the facilitator records it.

- **Quiet:** answer with one word or one sentence.
- **Curious:** explore a question without needing to settle it.
- **Vivid:** invent a brief scene, voice, or sensory detail.

Tone controls the form of the response, not its emotional content. A player
may change tone before sharing.

### 4. Offer a prompt

The facilitator chooses or adapts one original prompt from the current round:

| Round | Quiet | Curious | Vivid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | What detail asked for your attention first? | What might be just outside this card's frame? | Describe the sound that begins when the scene moves. |
| 2 | Name something the card seems to hold still. | What changes if one visible element trades places with another? | Give the scene a three-sentence before, during, and after. |
| 3 | What single word would you place beside this card? | What question would one figure ask another? | Speak one line in the voice of an object shown on the card. |
| 4 | Which edge, gap, or direction feels most noticeable? | What ordinary task could this card make newly interesting? | Move the scene to a different season and describe one change. |
| 5 | What would you title this moment? | What connection could this card make with an earlier draw? | End a tiny story with an image borrowed from the card. |

For a shorter session, use only the rows reached. A facilitator may write an
equally neutral follow-up in Prompt notes, but must not assign a personality,
future event, diagnosis, or fixed symbolic meaning to the card or player.

### 5. Share or pass

The player may speak, dictate a sentence for Reflection, write privately and
share nothing, or say `pass`. If they pass, leave Reflection empty unless they
ask the facilitator to record a neutral marker such as `Passed`.

Listeners may thank the player or repeat a phrase they appreciated. They do
not interpret the card for the player, correct the reflection, or turn the
response into advice.

### 6. Place the card

Leave the card face up until every player has completed the round. This creates
a temporary shared row of names and images without assigning relationships or
meanings to them.

## Complete a round

After everyone has drawn or passed:

1. Invite the group to notice one visual echo across the row, without
   interpreting any person.
2. Move all drawn cards to a face-down discard pile.
3. Advance the round by one and move the starting-player marker clockwise.
4. If the draw pile runs out, shuffle the discard pile and continue.

Do not advance the tracker after the final chosen round.

## Close the folio

At the end, each player may read one of their own reflections, name a tone they
enjoyed, or pass. The facilitator thanks the group, reminds everyone that the
cards were creative prompts rather than guidance or prediction, and returns
all cards to the deck.

Session notes should contain only details the group agreed to keep. Begin a
new session for another gathering so the earlier reflections are not silently
combined with a new group.
